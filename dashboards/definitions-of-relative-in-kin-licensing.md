title: Definitions of "relative" in kin licensing
description: Track which jurisdictions have implemented kin licensing standards
  and their definitions of "relative."
intro: >-
  Definitions of "relative" vary between states, with many excluding certain
  kinds of kin caregivers. Blood relatives and specific legal family members may
  qualify, but other people with deep, meaningful connections may not. As a
  result, youth may be placed with foster families instead of godparents, family
  friends, and trusted community members they already know.


  As states implement kin licensing standards, their definition of "relative" is particularly important. Broad definitions keep youth connected to their communities and supportive relationships.


  There's substantial variation in definitions among states that have implemented kin licensing standards. These are some of their broad definitions of "relative":


  * **12 states** don't limit blood, legal, or adoptive relationships by distance (or degree of consanguinity)

  * **10 states** recognize relationships defined by tribal customs

  * **19 states** include people with significant relationships to youth

  * **11 states** include people with significant relationships to youth's parents

  * **10 states** include people with significant relationships to youth's other family members


  Learn more about how to [implement kin licensing standards](https://childwelfareplaybook.com/kin-standards/).
