If a prospective foster parent lived in another state within the last 5 years,
their current state must check the child abuse and neglect registry from their
prior state before approving them as foster parents.

This sounds like a good idea. But in practice, inconsistent processes across states for fulfilling these requests are putting youth at risk, and cause some of the most significant delays in licensing (paying) foster parents, particularly for kinship caregivers who already have placement of youth but who do not receive any financial support until they are licensed.
