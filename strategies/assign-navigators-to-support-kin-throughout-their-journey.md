Kin caregivers face complex processes like licensing, court hearings, service referrals, and permanency planning. They often don't have a consistent person to help them navigate these processes. Many feel abandoned, overwhelmed, and afraid to ask for help because they worry it might lead to a youth being removed.

Provide kin with dedicated case managers or navigators who serve as their main point of contact, advocate for their needs, and guide them through processes.

## How to do this

**Create dedicated kin navigator or case manager roles.** These could be staff within your agency or contracted through child placing agencies. The key is that kin have one consistent person they can turn to throughout their journey.

**Make navigators the primary point of contact for kin.** Navigators should handle questions, address concerns, and coordinate with other staff on the kin's behalf. This gives kin a single person to call instead of trying to figure out who to contact for different issues. Reduce the number of different staff reaching out to kin, especially early in a case. When 15 to 20 different people contact kin separately for different purposes, caregivers get confused about who does what and miss critical connections for support.

**Maintain continuity when navigators change.** When a navigator leaves or transfers a case, make sure the new navigator receives complete case information and makes direct contact with the family. Brief the kin caregiver on who their new contact is and how to reach them.

**Train navigators to explain processes clearly.** Navigators should be able to explain what's happening, what to expect next, what options families have, and who else is involved in their case. Many kin don't understand the child welfare system and need someone to translate processes into plain language.

**Empower navigators to advocate for kin needs.** Navigators should be able to speak up when kin need something. This could be help with family dynamics, service referrals, or clarity about their rights. They should help facilitate agreements that work for both youth and caregivers.

**Inform kin about their rights and how to address conflicts.** Navigators should tell kin how to appeal decisions, how to advocate in court, where to file complaints if needed, and what offices to contact when conflicts arise. Kin shouldn't fear asking for help or asserting their needs.

**Pace support over time.** Kin often can't absorb everything they need to know in the first days of a placement. Coordinate mentor, case aide, and staff roles so support is sequenced. Provide the most critical information first, and introduce other supports as caregivers get further into the placement and have capacity to engage with them.

## This strategy in action

**Bloom Works** research on kin-first approaches and kin supports found that kin felt overwhelmed navigating complex processes without consistent support, and that reducing the number of contacts early in a case would improve clarity.
