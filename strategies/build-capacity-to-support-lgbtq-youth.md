To support LGBTQ+ youth, agency staff need to understand sexual orientation and gender identity. Provide training and ongoing support so staff can provide an affirming environment, effectively support these youth, and connect them to services.

## How to do this

### Train staff on how to support and affirm youth

**Bring in experts to train staff on sexual orientation and gender identity.** Cover topics like understanding LGBTQ+ identities, what gender dysphoria is, and the mental health impacts of misgendering youth. Try to include people with lived experience as trainers. Use small groups for better interaction. Create space for staff to ask questions and share hesitation without judgment. Hold training often and update it regularly as the field changes. If budget is a constraint, adapt existing training materials from LGBTQ+ organizations.

**Require training for all staff, not just caseworkers.** This includes leadership, managers, supervisors, attorneys, and anyone working with youth. Everyone needs this training – from leadership to frontline staff.

**Train leadership and supervisors to support their staff.** Train leaders and supervisors on how to coach caseworkers through questions and situations about sexual orientation and gender identity. Support ongoing learning in supervisor meetings.

**Give staff ongoing access to experts.** Staff will have questions after training ends. Make sure they can reach experts who can answer questions, help with specific situations, and advise on practice changes.

### Create a supportive culture

**Build a culture where mistakes are expected and corrected.** Staff will make missteps like using the wrong pronouns. When mistakes happen, guide staff on how to recover: apologize to the youth, understand what went wrong, and plan to do better.

**Encourage an environment of curiosity and self-education.** Staff won't know every term or concept right away, and that's okay. Create space for staff to learn without feeling threatened. Not knowing something isn't a misstep, it's an opportunity to learn.

### Let youth define their identities and determine disclosure

**Require staff to let youth take the lead.** Youth should define their own identities and decide what information to disclose and to whom. Train staff to ask youth what terms they use for themselves instead of applying labels, and to respect youth decisions about who knows what about their identity.

## This strategy in action

**Bloom Works** research found that staff from all backgrounds can build confidence in supporting LGBTQ+ youth with robust training from experts, frequent practice opportunities, and ongoing consultation.
