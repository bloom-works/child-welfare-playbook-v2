Start collecting data on sexual orientation and gender identity. This will ensure you can refer to youth correctly across staff and system handoffs, identify disparities, maintain confidentiality, and track whether LGBTQ+ youth are getting the care they need.

## How to do this

**Ask about and track identity and confidentiality preferences.** Youth should control who knows what about their identity. Build this into your initial conversations and update it as youth's comfort levels change.

**Train staff on how to ask questions about identity respectfully and naturally.** Staff should know how to have these conversations in ways that feel comfortable for youth and respect their privacy. Staff should ask all youth about their preferred names, pronouns, and disclosure preferences, not just those who appear to be LGBTQ+. Asking everyone normalizes the practice and avoids singling out specific youth. Staff should refer to everyone using their preferred name and pronoun.

**Collect data at appropriate times.** Ask about sexual orientation and gender identity when youth feel comfortable and you've built some trust. Explain why you're asking: so staff can refer to them correctly across handoffs (in court, at meetings, in service referrals) and connect them to appropriate services and support. It's critical that each staff member knows how to refer to youth so they aren't constantly correcting people.

**Update systems to track this data.** Work with IT to set up separate fields for sex, gender identity, pronouns, and sexual orientation. This may take time to implement, but advocate for these changes to properly support LGBTQ+ youth. Make sure your system can track both legal names and preferred names. If your system doesn't have structured fields for this data yet, collect this information in notes fields while advocating for proper data structure.

**Track who youth have told about their identity to protect their privacy.** Build in privacy protections so information is only shared with people the youth chose to tell. Your system should track what information youth have shared and with whom. This prevents staff from accidentally telling someone the youth isn't ready to tell.

**Look at patterns across different groups of youth to identify disparities.** Break down the data by age, race, and ethnicity to spot patterns. Review data regularly to identify gaps in services or placements for LGBTQ+ youth.

**Use the data to make service decisions and track outcomes.** Train staff to use this data when making decisions about services, placements, and programming to improve outcomes for LGBTQ+ youth.

## This strategy in action

**Bloom Works** research found that agencies need separate data fields for sex, gender identity, pronouns, and sexual orientation. And agencies need to use this information to make service decisions and track outcomes for LGBTQ+ youth.
