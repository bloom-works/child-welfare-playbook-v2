It can take years for prospective caregivers to go from thinking about fostering to submitting an application. But agencies often close inquiries after a few months of no response. This loses caregivers who are still interested but not ready yet.

Keep prospective caregivers in your system and offer ways to get involved even if they're not ready to proceed immediately. Maintain recruiter relationships throughout the process.

## How to do this

**Don't close inquiries after a few months of silence.** Keep prospective caregivers in your system and continue engaging them even if they're not ready to proceed immediately. People can take years to move from considering fostering to actually applying. If you close their inquiry after 6 months, you lose them.

**Start with smaller invitations to get people involved.** Don't expect prospective caregivers to commit immediately. Invite people to attend an information session, follow on social media, donate goods, volunteer at an event, or provide transportation. As people get more comfortable, invite them into bigger commitments like respite care and then placements. People who aren't ready to foster may stay engaged in other ways and eventually become foster caregivers.

**Offer a refresher instead of requiring caregivers to repeat the full licensing training.** If someone completes pre-service training but doesn't submit an application within a year, offer a shorter refresher course instead of making them repeat the full training. This removes an unnecessary barrier.

**Keep recruiters involved throughout the process.** When caregivers build a relationship with a recruiter and then that person disappears, it can break momentum. Keep recruiters engaged in training, home visits, or other activities instead of completely transitioning the relationship to licensing staff.

## This strategy in action

**Frederick County, MD** keeps families engaged even when they take time to complete applications. Many families take 6 or more months to apply. If a family completes pre-service training but doesn't submit an application within a year, they take a refresher. Frederick County also sends interested families a list of 12 ways to support youth without becoming foster caregiverparents. Recruiters conduct foster caregiverparent training, which helps families feel more comfortable as they start the process.

**Fairfax County, VA** keeps all inquiries open. They've found that families take an average of 2 to 3 years to move from inquiry to application. Recruitment team members join a family's initial home visit for a warm introduction to the licensor.

**Bloom Works** research on treatment foster care and foster families who provide stable placements found that many people take years from considering being a foster parent to submitting an application. Researchers recommended starting with awareness, then smaller commitments, and gradually building up to help develop a pipeline of prepared caregivers.
