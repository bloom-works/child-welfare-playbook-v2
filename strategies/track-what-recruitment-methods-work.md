Agencies often rely on the same recruitment avenues they've always used, like churches and community events. They may miss opportunities to build relationships with other faith communities, LGBTQ+ families, people with former foster care experience, or specialized education and health professionals.

Track referral sources and conversion rates (how many people move from one step to the next) so you can invest in methods that actually bring in licensed caregivers.

## How to do this

**Ask about referral sources during conversations, not just on forms.** Conversations give you richer and more detailed answers than written forms. People can explain how they heard about fostering in ways that reveal multiple touchpoints instead of picking one option from a list. When you do use forms, add detailed fields with "Other (please be specific)" options so prospective caregivers can describe how they found you in their own words.

**Track conversion rates at each step of the recruitment process.** Follow prospective caregivers as they learn about fostering, inquire about becoming caregivers, complete licensing requirements, and accept placements. In practice, steps like completing licensing requirements include many smaller sub-steps. Identify where prospective caregivers stop going through the process and why.

**Go beyond counting people reached at events.** Don't just track how many people attended a recruitment fair. Track how many of those attendees became inquiries, how many completed training, and how many got licensed. This tells you which events actually convert.

**Analyze the gap between youth needs and available caregivers.** Compare the needs of youth in care to the types of families you have available. You don't need expensive software. You can do this with spreadsheets. Use this analysis to identify kin and target your recruitment efforts.

## This strategy in action

**Bloom Works** research on recruiting foster families who provide stable placements found that agencies need better data on which recruitment methods actually work. By tracking referral sources and conversion rates, agencies can identify which investments pay off.
