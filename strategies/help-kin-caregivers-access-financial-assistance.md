Kin caregivers may face financial barriers that make it difficult to provide for youth in their care, even though they're willing and able to provide safe, loving homes. License kin caregivers regardless of their financial situation and connect them with financial assistance and support programs.

## How to do this

**License kin caregivers without financial requirements.** Don't require minimum income or financial stability to approve kin. Instead, focus on the caregiver's ability to meet the physical, emotional, medical, and educational needs of the youth. For kin, licensing typically happens after placement. Don't let financial requirements delay this process or create barriers to getting kin the supports that come with licensing.

**Connect kin caregivers to assistance at the start.** Provide a list of benefits that families may qualify for, or offer 1-on-1 application assistance. Examples include TANF, SNAP, unemployment insurance, social security, and VA benefits for those who qualify. For caregivers who can't access federal programs, connect them with local food banks, community assistance programs, charitable organizations, and other local resources.

**Provide application assistance that's customized to your area.** This includes providing translated materials and local program information.

**Continue the licensing process while families are working to access financial benefits.** You can complete many steps (like background checks and assessments) while you connect families to support programs.

**Keep providing support after you license caregivers.** Continue connecting families with resources and assistance programs even after licensing. This helps maintain stability and meet the youth's needs.

**Eliminate duplicative paperwork that creates barriers.** Review your licensing and approval process for unnecessary documentation requirements that make it harder for kin to get approved quickly. Streamline forms and reduce the number of steps kin must complete. Every additional form or requirement creates another opportunity for families to get stuck or give up.

**Provide support for families with low digital literacy.** Many kin may not be comfortable with online applications or digital systems. Offer in-person assistance, phone support, or paper alternatives for families who need them. Don't let digital barriers prevent kin from accessing financial support. Some families will need someone to sit with them and walk through applications step by step.

## This strategy in action

**Maryland** went from 33 forms to 3 when they implemented their new kinship standards.

**Indiana** and **Washington State** have implemented programs to help kin families access financial assistance during the licensing process. Washington also piloted a program to give kin assistance filling out application and licensing forms.

**Bloom Works** research found that excessive paperwork, digital-only systems, and income barriers prevented many willing kin from getting approved quickly. The research recommended streamlining paperwork, offering in-person support, and removing financial requirements to increase kin placements.
