Youth often have to reach a crisis before they can get the treatment foster care-style support they need. Youth in crisis often get placed in institutional care, and only later into the appropriate level of treatment foster care. And youth often lose their treatment-style support as soon as they stabilize, which can lead to another crisis.

Assess youth early and assign care teams before they reach crisis. Expand treatment foster care-style support to prevention settings and kin homes so youth and families can access support earlier. And continue providing this support after a crisis to help youth stay stable.

## How to do this

**Assess youth needs early and provide the right level of support right away.** Don't make youth wait for multiple placement disruptions or severe crises to qualify for specialized services. When needs emerge, provide the level of support those needs require. And when youth stabilize, don’t scale back the supports that helped them get there.

**Extend treatment foster care programs to kin homes.** Don't limit treatment foster care and other specialized supports to only non-kin placements. Kin caregivers caring for youth with high behavioral, emotional, or medical needs deserve the same level of support, training, and compensation that treatment foster caregivers receive.

**Offer treatment foster care-style services to families before you remove youth from their homes.** This means providing wrap-around services, intensive case aide support, and behavioral health support to families as early intervention to prevent youth from entering care.

## This strategy in action

**Bloom Works** research on treatment foster care found significant gaps. Youth need to experience escalating levels of crisis to receive specialized support. And that support is typically not extended to kin homes that are not licensed treatment foster care homes.
