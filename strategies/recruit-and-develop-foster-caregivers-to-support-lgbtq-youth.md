LGBTQ+ youth need affirming placements, but many agencies don't have enough foster caregivers ready to provide this kind of care. Some caregivers bring relevant lived experience with LGBTQ+ identities, while others can develop affirming skills through training and support.

Actively recruit caregivers who already have connections to LGBTQ+ communities. At the same time, give all willing caregivers the training and coaching they need to become excellent caregivers for these youth.

## How to do this

**Make supporting LGBTQ+ youth a priority in your recruitment messaging.** State clearly that this is a core organizational value. Don't wait until you train caregivers to bring this up. Make it part of how you talk about what it means to be a foster caregiver with your agency.

**Actively recruit LGBTQ+ people and couples as foster** **caregivers.** These families often have personal experience that makes them well-prepared to care for LGBTQ+ youth in foster care.

**Screen caregivers for their willingness to learn.** Look for caregivers who are open and willing to grow, even if they don't currently feel prepared to care for LGBTQ+ youth. Ask questions about their willingness to develop in this area and their goals as caregivers.

**Require all foster caregivers to commit to supporting LGBTQ+ youth.** Make this a licensing requirement, not just an expectation. Caregivers who aren't ready should receive training and support to build their skills, but caregivers who refuse to commit should not be approved. This protects all youth, including those who haven't come out as LGBTQ+ yet.

**Provide in-depth training for all caregivers, including kin.** Go beyond basic terminology. Help caregivers understand what it means to create environments where youth feel safe expressing themselves, including access to gender-affirming clothes and haircuts.

**Offer ongoing coaching and resources.** Not all caregivers will start with the same level of comfort or knowledge. Provide coaching when caregivers need help and connect them with additional resources.

**Partner with LGBTQ+ community organizations for caregiver development.** Work with community organizations that specialize in LGBTQ+ issues to help caregivers develop their skills. Connect families to LGBTQ+ foster family networks where they can learn from each other.

## This strategy in action

**Bloom Works** research found that "affirming families are made, not found." Any family can become supportive of LGBTQ+ youth with the right training and accountability, as long as they are willing and open to learning.
