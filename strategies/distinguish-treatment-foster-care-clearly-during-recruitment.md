Treatment foster care is family-based care for youth with significant behavioral health, mental health, or medical needs. Treatment foster caregivers get more intensive training, clinical support, and financial support. 

Prospective caregivers often don't know the difference between treatment foster care and traditional foster care. This leads to mismatched expectations and poor fits. When recruitment materials don't clearly explain what makes treatment foster care different, caregivers sign up without understanding what they're committing to.

Clearly distinguish treatment foster care from traditional foster care in all recruitment materials. Explain youth needs, training, support, compensation, and time commitments so caregivers can make informed choices.

## How to do this

**Explain what makes treatment foster care different from traditional foster care.** Don't assume caregivers understand the distinction. In all recruitment materials, explain the level of youth needs, training provided, ongoing support available, compensation differences, and time commitments. Be specific and honest about what the program involves.

**Help caregivers understand which program fits their skills, availability, and goals.** Make sure caregivers can make informed choices about which program to pursue. Don't let caregivers accidentally sign up for the wrong program because they didn't understand the differences.

**Address the stigma and fear about youth needs.** Some prospective caregivers are interested in treatment foster care but are scared off by assumptions about the youth they'd be caring for. Be honest and forthcoming about youth needs. But also help caregivers see that with the right training and support, they can provide this level of care.

**Target specialized populations in your recruitment.** Education and healthcare specialists often have skills that transfer well to treatment foster care. And people who have similar lived experiences as youth in treatment foster care may be more motivated to accept placements. This includes people who have a family history with child welfare involvement. Recruit in these communities intentionally.

**Create roles for treatment foster care caregivers in recruitment.** Let caregivers who are already in the program talk to prospective caregivers about their experiences. This helps new caregivers understand what to expect in concrete terms.

## This strategy in action

**Bloom Works** research on treatment foster care found that many caregivers didn't know they were signing up for treatment foster care instead of traditional foster care. Clearer distinctions in recruitment materials would help recruit the right families and improve retention.
