While foster caregivers may get legal support through foster networks or child placing agencies, kin caregivers often navigate permanency and other complex legal processes without support. Kin are often underprepared for court and lack a full understanding of the process and their options.

Connect kin with free or low-cost legal services. Work with court partners to support kin for the full lifecycle of their case, through permanency. 

## How to do this

**Partner with legal clinics or legal aid organizations to provide free or low-cost legal services.** Identify organizations that can provide independent legal counsel to kin caregivers throughout dependency and permanency planning. Legal aid organizations, law school clinics, and pro bono programs often serve families who can't afford private attorneys. Make sure kin caregivers have the same access to legal and community resources that foster caregivers receive through foster parent networks or child placing agencies.

**Make referrals to legal services early in the process.** Don't wait until permanency hearings are imminent. Connect kin with legal support before they navigate legal proceedings so they understand their rights and options from the start.

**Talk to kin early and often about permanency possibilities.** Explain early that case plan goals often change. Kin caregivers are often under the impression that the placement will be short-term and then feel surprised by conversations about long-term planning. Hold early conversations about how the direction of cases can change, so caregivers can prepare.

**Prepare kin for court.** Prepare kin caregivers and youth for what to expect in court. Remember that this may be the first time they’re entering a courtroom. Identify accessibility needs like sign language, subtitles, interpreters, and language access.

**Work with court partners to support kin placement and permanency.** Staff recommendations heavily influence how judges make decisions. Train staff to advocate for kin placement and permanency decisions and present information effectively in court. Make sure caseworkers consistently include all interested kin in their court reports. Consider sharing kin bench cards with judges that summarize key elements of a kin-friendly courtroom. 

## This strategy in action

**Legal Advice and Referral for Kinship Care (LAARK)** in Washington State provides independent legal counsel primarily for kin caregivers.

**Bloom Works** conducted research on kin-first approaches in Washington State and permanency planning with kin caregivers in Oklahoma. Caregivers consistently reported that access to legal support made a significant positive difference.
