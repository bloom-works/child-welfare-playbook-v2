Foster and kin caregivers often feel isolated and lack peer support to help them through the challenges of caregiving. Kin caregivers in particular may lack access to advocacy groups and agency-led connections. Without structured opportunities to connect, caregivers struggle alone with problems that peer networks could help solve.

Create structured peer support networks where caregivers can connect, share experiences, and learn from each other. This reduces isolation and improves retention for foster caregivers and stability for kin caregivers.

## How to do this

**Develop structured networks where foster and kin caregivers support each other.** These networks can take many forms, including in-person or virtual support groups, online forums, and buddy systems. They can help caregivers share resources to support each other and the youth in their care, and facilitate respite care arrangements between families. 

**Plan activities for caregivers to share experiences and learn from each other.** Include time for caregivers to ask questions and talk about what's working, what's challenging, and what strategies they've found helpful. Let caregivers learn from each other's successes and failures. Regular connection and structured focus around a specific topic helps caregivers feel less isolated and more capable of handling challenges.

**Create opportunities for caregivers to take on leadership roles over time.** Let experienced caregivers mentor newer caregivers, lead support groups, or take on other leadership roles in the network. This gives families ways to contribute and grow while supporting others.

**Provide compensation for mentoring roles and community building activities.** Pay caregivers for the time they spend mentoring other caregivers or facilitating peer support activities. This recognizes the value of their work and makes it more sustainable.

## This strategy in action

**Bloom Works** research on foster families who provide stable placements found that peer support networks help caregivers feel less isolated and more capable of handling challenges.

**DC CFSA's** BOND (Building Our Network of Diversity) model provides compensation for mentoring roles and creates opportunities for families to support each other and take on leadership roles over time. BOND leads facilitate community building activities and help families navigate processes.

**Alliance CaRES** (Caregiver Retention, Education and Support) provides compensation for peer mentors to support caregivers in Washington State.
