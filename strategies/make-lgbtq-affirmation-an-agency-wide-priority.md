Training staff is important, but it's not enough on its own. Agencies that treat LGBTQ+ affirmation as just a training topic don't see lasting change. Without leadership commitment, dedicated funding, and clear policies, affirmation stays inconsistent and dependent on individual staff willingness.

Make LGBTQ+ affirmation a core organizational goal. Create policies, fund initiatives, and hold your agency and its partners accountable.

## How to do this

**Name LGBTQ+ affirmation as a leadership priority.** Don't collapse it into general diversity or anti-discrimination work. LGBTQ+ affirmation has specific needs that get lost when it's treated as a subset of broader equity efforts. Give it its own focus, speak about it frequently, and make it visible in your agency's priorities.

**Update your agency's definition of safety to include LGBTQ+ affirmation.** If your definition of safety doesn't specifically reference sexual orientation, gender identity, and gender expression, it's incomplete. A general reference isn't enough. Be specific about what affirmation looks like and what your agency expects.

**Require all foster caregivers to commit to affirming LGBTQ+ youth.** Make this a condition of approval. Caregivers who aren't ready should get training and support to build their skills, but those who refuse to commit should not be approved. This protects all youth, including those who haven't come out as LGBTQ+ yet.

**Create youth-led room assignment policies in congregate care.** Room assignments should take sexual orientation and gender identity into account, not just sex assigned at birth. Decisions should be led by the youth whenever possible.

**Create a protocol for conversations about sexual orientation and gender identity.** Staff should have clear guidance on how to hold these conversations with youth at different ages and developmental levels.

**Integrate kin-first and LGBTQ+ affirmation with nuance.** Don't treat these as competing priorities. Create policy that recognizes both. For example, if a youth trusts kin who aren't yet fully affirming, policy should allow for placement with a plan to support that family's growth rather than ruling them out entirely. This requires thoughtful case-by-case judgment, not a blanket either/or rule.

**Extend affirmation support to families of origin.** When reunification is the goal, parents may need help understanding and affirming a youth's identity. Offer education, training, and support to families of origin in relevant languages to support both reunification and prevention.

**Require affirming approaches across your partner ecosystem.** Child placing agencies, service providers, community organizations, courts, and legal advocates that work with your agency should meet the same standard of LGBTQ+ competence. Include affirmation requirements in contracts and hold partners accountable.

**Seek dedicated funding for affirmation initiatives.** Training, consultation, community partnerships, and youth programs all cost money. Pursue funding specifically for LGBTQ+ affirmation work rather than hoping it fits into existing budgets.

**Assign trained supervisors to track and coach on affirmation.** Don't rely on one-time training alone. Give supervisors specific responsibility for coaching staff on LGBTQ+ affirmation and tracking how it's going in practice.

## This strategy in action

**Bloom Works** developed the queer affirmation maturity model from research including interviews with LGBTQ+ youth, agency staff, foster families, and queer education experts. The research found that agencies with leadership buy-in to queer affirmation have higher uptake and impact across all areas.
