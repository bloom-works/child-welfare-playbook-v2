With the right support and training, foster caregivers can develop their skills and grow into caring for a wider range of youth needs. But case management often focuses on compliance, instead of responding to how caregivers change and grow over time. 

Create progression systems that help foster caregivers develop skills over time. Build these pathways into your licensing and renewal processes. Adjust the support you provide as caregiver and youth needs change.

## How to do this

**Create tiered progression systems that show how foster caregivers can grow.** Map out how foster caregivers can grow from caring for youth with basic needs to caring for youth with more complex needs. Make this progression visible so both caseworkers and foster caregivers can understand what's possible.

**Match youth to where foster caregivers are in their progression.** Don't expect new foster caregivers to take the most challenging placements right away. Let them build skills and confidence by placing youth with less complex needs first. As caregivers gain experience, gradually place youth with more complex needs who match their developing skills.

**Use renewal conversations and other touchpoints as development check-ins.** Don't use licensing renewals and other case management touchpoints only for compliance checks. Take every opportunity to talk about how the foster caregiver has grown, what new skills they've developed, and whether they're interested in different types of placements. Help them set goals for youth needs they may want to support in the future. And adjust the support you offer based on their current needs and future goals.

**Recognize that even experienced caregivers may need more support at different times.** Caregivers who have been fostering successfully for years might need intensive support to care for youth with needs they haven't encountered before. Don't assume experienced caregivers don't need help.

## This strategy in action

**Bloom Works** research on treatment foster care and foster families who provide stable placements found that programs treating caregivers as developing over time tend to see stronger retention than those focused primarily on compliance. This research found that families need clear, gradual progression systems instead of systems that expect them to handle the highest-needs youth right away. 

**Arkansas** uses a 3-tier treatment foster care system where families progress through tiers based on the level of care coordination and services youth need.

**Indiana** uses 4 CANS (Child and Adolescent Needs and Strengths) assessment levels that determine reimbursement rates and case manager contact frequency.
