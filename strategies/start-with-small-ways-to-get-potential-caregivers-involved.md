Agreeing to a first placement is a big commitment. If the focus of your outreach always goes straight to licensing and placements, potential foster caregivers may find the idea too overwhelming. Or they may feel pressured to accept placements before they’re ready.

Start with small opportunities for people to get involved in the foster system. Then provide chances for them to take on increasing levels of responsibility and commitment. 

## How to do this

**Offer small, low-stakes volunteer opportunities.** In your initial outreach to potential foster caregivers, start with smaller asks like donating goods, volunteering at an event, or providing transportation. 

**Gradually increase the level of commitment.** It may be hard for people to jump straight from a few volunteer hours to a first placement. Scaffold this progression by asking them to take on smaller commitments, like respite care.

**Give training credit for volunteer hours.** Create a recruitment model where volunteering with youth in foster care counts towards training hours. 

**Separate learning opportunities from licensing.** Disentangle learning from licensing, so people can explore the idea of fostering without feeling pressure to accept a placement before they're ready. But make sure learning experiences will count towards licensing requirements if and when people decide to pursue licensing.

## This strategy in action

**Bloom Works** research on treatment foster care and foster families who provide stable placements recommended separating learning from licensure and starting with smaller volunteer commitments to help develop a pipeline of potential foster caregivers.
