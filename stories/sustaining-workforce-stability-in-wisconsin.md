## The challenge: A staffing cliff

In early 2021, Children’s Wisconsin Child Welfare (CWCW) encountered an unprecedented workforce crisis. Annualized staff turnover peaked at an unsustainable 41%, driving vacancy rates to 19% and leaving over half of the remaining active case managers carrying workloads significantly exceeding contractually defined safety thresholds.

## The intervention: Urgent action from leadership

To stabilize frontline operations and safely protect continuity of care for Wisconsin's families, CWCW leadership deployed a series of targeted tactical strategies. Rather than relying on a single mechanism, leadership intervened simultaneously across these focal areas:

* **Workload redesign:** Hired specialized case aides and mobilized volunteers to offload extensive administrative burdens from frontline caseworkers
* **Hybrid flexibility:** Formalized a trust-based office-by-need work model, shifting professional autonomy and scheduling geography back to the staff
* **Compensation adjustments:** Executed a 19% market floor baseline salary adjustment and instituted immediate surge pay for hours worked beyond full-time limits

## The evaluation: Identifying what worked

Following the successful stabilization of the workforce, the Institute for Child and Family Well-being partnered with CWCW to conduct a rigorous, reflective evaluation to determine which strategies were most effective and why. 

The evaluation synthesized three distinct data strands: Quantitative workforce metrics, retrospective strategy mapping of leadership intervention timing, and the qualitative lived experiences of frontline staff captured through discovery tools.

> "I had a caseload of 23 and now it's 15, so it's a lot easier to manage."
>
> — Case manager

The evaluation successfully validated that while compensation was important, the relational elements, such as supportive supervision and flexible autonomy, served as the long-term fuel that sustained the workforce.

## The result: 5 pillars of stability

The findings from the evaluation codified the successful turnaround into 5 interdependent pillars of stability, establishing a clear conceptual blueprint for long-term organizational health.

1. **Supportive and responsive leadership:** Operationalizing responsive visibility and proactive advocacy that prioritizes worker personhood over simple task production
2. **Hybrid autonomy and work-life flexibility:** Transitioning to trust-based scheduling architectures that grant staff authentic control over professional geography
3. **Workload management and caseload equilibrium:** Enforcing strict, non-negotiable adherence to safety thresholds (~15-child limit) to protect the clinical depth of the work
4. **Relational culture and holistic wellbeing:** Embedding peer-to-peer validation networks, intentional psychological safety, and direct, visible recognition touchpoints into daily workflows
5. **Competitive compensation and value recognition:** Sustaining structural adjustments to anchor employee value and establish a stable economic baseline

By implementing a cross section of these organizational strategies, the program shifted from crisis to workforce recovery.
